Prime walk at the zeta zeros

Turn every prime power n by the angle t·ln n, give it the length Λ(n)/√n, and chain the arrows head to tail. At most t the walk tangles up near where it started. Slide t onto 14.1347 and it straightens into a line.

Wt(N) = Σn ≤ N Λ(n)·n−1/2·e−it ln n − (N1/2 − it − 1)/(1/2 − it)

What you are seeing

  • Between zeros the arrows keep changing direction and the walk stays in a small tangle.
  • Near a zero γ the walk follows an arc of a circle through the start point, with radius 1/|t − γ|. The closer you get, the bigger and flatter the arc.
  • At a zero the circle opens into a straight line pointing left, growing like ln N. This is the prime walk resonating with the zero.

Why this happens

The sum is the Dirichlet series for −ζ′/ζ(s) on the line s = 1/2 + it. By the explicit formula, each zero ρ = 1/2 + iγ adds a term −Nρ−s/(ρ − s), which traces a circle of radius 1/|γ − t| as N grows and becomes −ln N when t = γ.

The subtracted term removes the pole of ζ at s = 1. Untick the box to see it: a spiral of radius about √N/t that hides everything else.
